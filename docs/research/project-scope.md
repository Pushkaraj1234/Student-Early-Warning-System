# Project Scope — Student Early Warning & Intervention System (SEWS)

> Status: **DRAFT v0.1** (2026-09-24). Nothing described here is implemented yet.
> Items marked **[OPEN]** are unresolved decisions. They are listed in §9 and must be resolved before the related work starts.

## 1. Purpose

SEWS is a mobile-first student success platform. It:

1. **Identifies early signals** that a student may have academic difficulty later in the term.
2. **Explains** the main factors behind each signal.
3. **Recommends** interventions, which a qualified human reviews, chooses and records.

SEWS supports human judgement. It does **not** make decisions about students on its own.

## 2. Non-negotiable framing

- A risk score is a **probabilistic estimate from a statistical model**. It is not a guaranteed outcome, a diagnosis or a label of ability.
- The UI, API responses and research outputs must never describe a prediction as certain ("will fail", "is a dropout").
  Use wording such as "elevated risk signal" or "model estimates a higher-than-usual likelihood of…".
- Explanations (e.g. SHAP attributions) describe **what drove the model's output**. They are **not causal** statements about the student.
- No automated punitive action (such as detention, loss of eligibility or disciplinary referral) may ever be triggered by a model output.

## 3. In scope

| Area | In scope |
|---|---|
| Mobile app (Flutter) | Sign-in; role-based dashboards; list of students flagged for review; student detail with explanation; intervention logging and follow-up |
| Backend (Supabase) | Auth, PostgreSQL schema, Row Level Security (RLS), Storage for permitted documents, Realtime for alert updates |
| ML service (FastAPI) | Serving versioned models; batch and on-demand scoring; returning explanations |
| ML research (Python) | Leakage-safe training and evaluation on benchmark, synthetic and (if approved) institutional data |
| Governance | Model/dataset/feature versioning, audit logging, consent records, model cards |

## 4. Out of scope (for this project version)

- Admissions, grading, scholarship or disciplinary decisions.
- Replacing an LMS, ERP or student information system. SEWS reads from them; it does not replace them.
- Selling, sharing or commercially reusing student data with third parties.
- Facial recognition, location tracking, device or social-media monitoring, or any biometric data.
- Claims that the model generalises to Indian students before it has been validated on Indian institutional data (see §6).

## 5. Intended users (proposed — **[OPEN]** until confirmed)

| Role | Proposed capability |
|---|---|
| Institution admin | Manage users, roles, terms, courses and data imports for **one** institution |
| Mentor / faculty advisor | See **assigned** students only, including risk signals and explanations; log interventions |
| Counsellor | See students referred to them; log interventions and outcomes |
| Student | **[OPEN]** Whether students see their own risk score (see research-questions RQ7 and the ethics notes) |
| Parent / guardian | **[OPEN]** Not in scope unless the target group includes minors and consent rules require it |
| Researcher | Access to **de-identified** exports only. No access to the production app |

## 6. Data provenance classes

Every dataset used anywhere in the project must carry exactly one of these labels. The labels must never be mixed up or misdescribed.

| Class | Meaning | Allowed claims |
|---|---|---|
| `benchmark` | Public research datasets, typically **non-Indian** (e.g. the UK Open University Learning Analytics Dataset, the UCI Portuguese student datasets) | Method development and comparison only. **No** claims about Indian students |
| `synthetic` | Generated by project code | Pipeline and UI testing only. **No** claims about real student behaviour or model accuracy |
| `institutional` | Real data from a partner Indian institution, under a signed data-sharing agreement, ethics approval and valid consent | The only basis for claims about the partner institution. Generalising beyond that institution needs further validation |

| Phase | Deliverable |

## 8. Success criteria (project level)

- All exposed tables have RLS enabled and policies covered by automated tests.
- Every prediction row records its model version, feature version, dataset version and scoring timestamp.
- ML results are reported on **held-out, out-of-time** data, with baselines, calibration and subgroup analysis.
- No secrets are present in the Flutter client or in version control.

## 9. Decisions made (2026-09-24, by the project owner)

- **Target population:** Indian higher education (college/university students, assumed 18+), UGC CBCS
  10-point grades (O, A+, A, B+, B, C, P, F, Ab), semesters, SGPA/CGPA, one current mentor per student.
- **Benchmark ML target:** Fail or Withdrawn = at risk (OULAD); students who withdrew before a cutoff are excluded.
- **Student visibility (V1 brief):** students see their own risk **level** (Stable/Watch/Elevated/High),
  its stored explanation factors and recommended actions — not the raw probability and not staff notes.
- **Hosted project:** changes are verified locally first; nothing is applied to `rzekfmfuskknadrchkhg`
  until the owner confirms.

## 10. Open decisions (must be resolved by the project owner)

1. **Target population**: school students (who may be under 18) or higher-education students. This affects consent requirements under India's DPDP Act, 2023 (see the security model).
2. **Definition of "academic difficulty"** (the prediction label). See `problem-definition.md` §3.
3. **Decision points and prediction horizon**. For example: week 4 of the term, predicting the end-of-term outcome.
4. **Partner institution and data availability**: which systems hold attendance, marks and LMS activity, and whether ethics approval exists.
5. **Student visibility of their own scores.**
6. **Hosting location of the FastAPI service** and data-residency requirements.
7. **Supabase project region and current schema**. Neither could be verified during inspection (see the architecture assessment).
